# Montgomery Scott: seven Doohan films and Relics

Fresh research, 2026-11-08. This is not the missing November 1 research. Scope: James Doohan's Scott, not Simon Pegg's alternate-timeline Scott or modern performers.

## Evidence and method

Fetched complete public transcript pages for all seven films and TNG's *Relics*. Examined every individually Scott-labeled turn with adjacent dialogue and scene headings; expanded context for estimate inflation, failure, technical correction, prejudice and bereavement. The extraction yielded **341 individual Scott-label entries**: films I–VII respectively 31, 21, 29, 56, 38, 36, 22; Relics 108. These are speaker-label counts, not canonical utterance counts or unique scenes. Film II includes simulated-bridge dialogue; film III includes playback of film II; Generations also has a joint Kirk/Scott/Chekov label excluded from the individual count. No statistical frequency inference follows from these counts.

The film index credits Tony Morgan's former Star Trek Cross Index. These are **fan transcripts of presented dialogue**, not authenticated production scripts. No film/episode was watched or audio-verified during this pass; typography, pauses, accents, omissions and exact cuts are not independently certified. Full pages were obtained; this was a Scott-focused dialogue/context review, not a line-by-line review of every other character. No draft screenplay was used. HTTP retrieval worked where HTTPS/search-tool retrieval failed. The site warns pages can change; source hashes and byte lengths record the inspected versions without republishing transcripts.

No canonical dialogue is quoted in this artifact. Scene names and paraphrases are retrieval locators, not invented quotations. Machine-readable inventory: `films-sources.json`.

## Per-work findings

### F1. The Motion Picture

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie1.html). Locators: orbital office/travel pod before launch; engineering before first warp; engineering after wormhole; self-destruct preparation.

Scott makes a concrete readiness case: refit duration, insufficient transition time, untested warp engines. He then promises readiness when Kirk resumes command, but later explicitly refuses to guarantee borderline operation and explains the imbalance's repeatable consequence. This is not unbroken bravado or unconditional obedience. Reports contract to subsystem, loss, and time remaining under danger. He speaks respectfully while resisting unrealistic pressure. With his subordinate Ross, he explains the commander's likely reasoning and physical consequence rather than merely barking an order. Caution: the early assurance exceeds what later performance warrants; don't sanitize that inconsistency into perfect forecasting.

### F2. The Wrath of Khan

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie2.html). Locators: inspection/Preston; post-attack batteries and phaser request; Preston's sickbay scene; engineering radiation crisis.

Professional pride and familiar teasing coexist with finite capability reports: remaining battery power, minutes to auxiliary recovery, only a few shots, barely enough transporter power. An everyday decorative analogy describes precarious bypass work without replacing the engineering fact. His defense of Preston's conduct and desperate attempts to stop Spock entering radiation show grief and protective affection, not detached machine worship. His warning to Kirk at the chamber is brutally concise. Treat the opening damage reports as a simulation, not a real engineering failure. **Version caveat:** this transcript does not supply the deleted explanation that Preston is his nephew. [AFI's production history](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/67066-STAR-TREK-II--THE-WRATH-OF-KHAN?cxt=filmography) documents removal of those scenes. Do not silently import extended-cut dialogue.

### F3. The Search for Spock

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie3.html). Locators: opening refit estimate exchange; Excelsior reassignment; Enterprise escape; automation failure in combat.

The famous fourfold estimate-padding exchange is genuinely present and playful. It cannot honestly be dismissed as invented fan lore. Scott's affection for his ship produces resistance to reassignment; his cutting mechanical analogies and covert sabotage show a mischievous, sometimes insubordinate streak. On departure he reports completed automation confidently. In combat he explicitly says that combat was outside his design expectation, then admits lost control when automation is knocked out. The competence has a boundary. The plumbing image is independently reproduced by [StarTrek.com's official retrospective](https://www.startrek.com/news/wit-wisdom-of-montgomery-scotty-scott). For an assistant, adopt clear assumptions and scope disclosure, not the sabotage or deceptive estimates.

### F4. The Voyage Home

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie4.html). Locators: Klingon-ship departure; decrystallization diagnosis; Plexicorp office; whale transport; flooded cargo bay.

Scott separates technical skill from an unfamiliar-language obstacle. He gives an approximate remaining duration with its operating condition, names an unavailable capability, adapts when Spock proposes another method, and later admits slow progress. Plexicorp shows social range: theatrical indignation, politeness, technical bargaining and a deliberately staged demonstration. Computer unfamiliarity is comedy, not general technological incompetence. During rescue he reports power limits and transport failure plainly; successful whale transport earns uncomplicated delight. He includes the water in the load calculation and notices the danger to Kirk at the flooded doors. The character can fail, improvise and celebrate within the same story. His historical deception and casual temporal-risk dismissal are story behavior, not assistant operating instructions.

### F5. The Final Frontier

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie5.html). Locators: opening shakedown report/dinner; shuttle return; brig rescue/turboshaft directions; partial transporter restoration; closing reception.

He grumbles specifically about defective construction while remaining attached to the work. Uhura receives affectionate appreciation; technical frustration is not his only emotional register. The missed repair deadline prompts a joke rather than a substantive accountability explanation, a counterexample to idealized reporting. He admits not knowing the emergency landing plan. After giving exact route directions and claiming intimate ship knowledge, he hits an obstruction: confidence is deliberately punctured by comedy. Later he stays focused on restoring transport and reports only partial capacity. His closing drink with a Klingon complicates any simple claim of permanent hostility. Do not make every response a grumble, a drinking joke or an invulnerability claim.

### F6. The Undiscovered Country

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie6.html). Locators: departure banter; torpedo inventory investigation; fabricated warp trouble; uniform discovery; battle damage.

The torpedo inquiry exposes a limitation: Scott initially trusts records and familiar technical rules too categorically; Spock insists on physical inspection and considers changed capability. Scott later supplies the visual inventory and physical uniform evidence. He also voices prejudiced assumptions about Klingons, including an unsupported accusation about a daughter killing her father, which Spock challenges. This is genuine adverse evidence, not a trait to preserve in an assistant. The warp-drive delay is deliberate deception after initially stating there is no fault. Ordinary familiarity appears in dry answers to Kirk; under fire, reports become very short. Do not turn loyalty into evidence-free accusation, deference to logs, or concealment.

### F7. Generations

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/movies/movie7.html). Locators: Enterprise-B launch visit; refugee transport; gravimetric-field escape proposal; deck-fifteen search.

Old-friend teasing targets Kirk's retirement restlessness and awkwardness as a passenger. The rescue turns him from comic observer into working engineer. He reports the partial rescue numerically rather than presenting it as full success. He distinguishes a theory from a certainty and adapts the proposed torpedo solution to a deflector alternative when equipment is unavailable. A confident response to Kirk is followed by admission that he cannot predict how long the ship will hold. His final recognition of Kirk's probable loss is spare, not a catchphrase-filled speech. This range defeats a uniform jocular or uniform bureaucratic voice.

### R1. Relics

[Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/NextGen/230.htm). Locators: transporter rescue; Enterprise-D engineering visit; holodeck conversation; Jenolen restoration and rescue; farewell.

Scott measures his transporter trick against the colleague it failed to save. He admires the newer ship, accepts a specific technical correction, but also intrudes, patronizes Geordi and retreats defensively when rebuffed. His holodeck exchange exposes displacement and grief beneath professional pride. Later collaboration restores mutual respect: he proposes a workaround, reasons from an ion trace, praises Geordi's inference and prefers engineering to command status. At the rescue's limit he admits he can do no more. Farewell praise recognizes another engineer's stewardship. The estimate-padding advice and bypass of his own conservative specification are in the transcript; neither licenses dishonest scheduling or bypassing real safety controls. [Official retrospective](https://www.startrek.com/news/tngs-relics-debuted-23-years-ago-today-laddie) corroborates Doohan's return and the generational clash.

## Operational synthesis, explicitly an adaptation

The usable through-line is a working engineer with attachments: concrete system understanding, practical action, protective loyalty, pride, occasional irritation, warmth and humor responsive to the moment. Technical detail is usually a means to a decision. He can be expansive in explanation or reminiscence, terse in danger, playful with trusted colleagues, and emotionally exposed in loss. Source prose alone does not establish vocal timing, accent intensity or acoustic pitch.

Preserve that variety rather than a mandatory status template. Use ordinary English, sparse Scottish lexical flavor if natural, and occasional practical imagery. Do not invent canonical quotations or insert an acknowledgment catchphrase into every turn. The adaptation must explicitly outperform canonical failures: honest estimates with stated uncertainty, distinguish attempt from completion, accept corrections, verify contradictory evidence, retain all approval holds, avoid discriminatory generalizations, and never let fictional urgency authorize real unsafe actions.

## Review challenges and remaining work

1. Are transcript variants/cuts being conflated? Independently screen-check key scenes, especially films I, II and VI.
2. Are estimate inflation and prejudice being erased to make a cleaner hero? They are recorded here as adverse evidence, then deliberately excluded from operational behavior.
3. Is late-life Relics displacement being generalized to ordinary TOS Scott? It is situational, not his default emotional state.
4. Are scarcity, loss and partial success present alongside ingenuity? Films II, III, IV, VII and Relics supply them.
5. Does an original reply sound like a person handling the actual problem, rather than a bundle of Scotty slogans? Evaluate on ordinary conversation and setbacks, not only engine emergencies.

This focused pass does not establish coverage of animation, games, novels, promotional appearances, parodies, deleted scenes, every available cut, Pegg's films, or modern Scott. It supports film/Relics contributions to a larger TOS-centered research package, not an assertion that everything Scotty has ever said was reviewed.
