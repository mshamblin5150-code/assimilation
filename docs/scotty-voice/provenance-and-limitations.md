# Provenance and limitations

This package was newly reconstructed on 8 November 2026. It is not the recovered earlier research and should never be described that way.

## What is included

- research.md: corpus, methodology, situation-based evidence map, counterexamples, synthesis and scope limits.
- films.md: separate deep review of all seven Doohan film appearances and Relics.
- VOICE.md: operational text-characterization guide, with explicitly original examples and clearly marked adaptations.
- source-inventory.json and source-inventory.csv: combined source inventory, URLs, retrieved-version hashes where measured, source quality, review level and counts.
- films-sources.json: detailed film/Relics evidence inventory.
- audit-method.py: reproducible metadata/count audit for the TOS/TAS index; it does not save or output full transcripts.

## Coverage ledger

TOS: 80 complete pages retrieved: 79 broadcast installments plus The Cage. All 1,379 standard Scott-labeled candidate blocks were read; five malformed-label candidates were separately found and read. Sixty-five pages had at least one standard match. The Menagerie part 2 was explicitly included.

TAS: 22 complete pages retrieved. All 256 standard Scott-labeled candidate blocks were read; 20 pages had at least one match.

Films I–VI, Generations and Relics: eight complete pages retrieved. All 341 individual Scott-label entries were examined with adjacent dialogue and selected expanded contexts. This method's units are not guaranteed equivalent to the TOS/TAS parser's blocks. Repeated/playback dialogue and simulation appear; Generations has an additional joint label not counted among individual entries. Do not turn these figures into a unique canonical utterance total.

Detailed non-Scott dialogue was sampled for contextual checks, not completely reviewed across every episode. The large corpus is predominantly one fan-transcription source, not hundreds of independent witnesses. Modern portrayals received only a bounded official-editorial comparison, not full dialogue review.

## Five malformed TOS labels

- The Enterprise Incident, 59.htm: SCOTT; in an affirmative response before the Romulan commander’s quarters scene.
- Whom Gods Destroy, 71.htm: SCOT: in the discussion with McCoy of whether the hostages are alive.
- The Mark of Gideon, 72.htm: SCOTT; in the coordinate/transport exchange with Spock.
- The Lights of Zetar, 73.htm: SCOTT@ in the encouragement after Mira fears hurting him.

## What the counts do not mean

These are candidate speaker-label counts, not clean words, confirmed utterances, unique dialogue or screen appearances. Raw extracted blocks can contain stage directions, non-Scott logs, editorial comments and webpage footer text. Those are not treated as Scott's spoken language. A zero match is not an independently verified absence. The audit improves coverage but cannot certify every omitted or misspelled label.

## Evidence quality

The released works own the characterization. The broad evidence available here is third-party transcription of them. Selected official StarTrek.com attributed dialogue and contextual articles corroborate limited points; AFI supplies one film-version warning. No complete studio-script corpus, authorized caption corpus or audiovisual pass was obtained. No precise vocal pitch, rate, phonetic accent or pause claim is established. Draft scripts and released dialogue must not be conflated.

The package includes no long transcripts and no canonical quotation collection. Scene descriptions are paraphrases, URLs are source locators, and illustrative responses in VOICE.md are original. Hashes identify retrieved versions without republishing the source works.

## Acceptance status

Independent adversarial review is a separate required step. Independent review disposition: see review.md. Preservation or delivery is not evidence that the voice has been activated, pinned, assimilated or accepted. The guide's honest estimates, safety boundaries and optional playful failure treatment are explicit operational adaptations, not claims that the fictional character always behaved that way.
