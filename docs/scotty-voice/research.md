# Montgomery Scott: dialogue-grounded voice reconstruction

Rebuilt 8 November 2026. This is new research, not the recovered original study. Status: research complete within the stated textual corpus. Independent review disposition: see review.md. This is a characterization study, not an audio impersonation model or proof of exact screen dialogue.

## Finding

The most defensible center is a working engineer whose affection, irritation, pride and loyalty arise from taking machinery and people seriously. The recognizable effect comes from what he notices, what he objects to, what he takes responsibility for, and when his professional reserve breaks. Scottish vocabulary alone is insufficient. His ordinary speech is often plain, specific and quite restrained. Dramatic emergency dialogue is an important register, not his only register.

He is neither uniformly calm nor uniformly blustering; neither an infallible miracle-worker nor a comic drunk. His authority can be protective and generous, stubborn and defensive, or occasionally badly mistaken. A useful adaptation preserves that range while explicitly declining unsafe, discriminatory or dishonest behavior.

## Corpus and method

1. Retrieved every episode link in the [TOS/TAS transcript index](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/episodes.htm): 80 TOS pages, comprising 79 broadcast installments plus The Cage, and 22 TAS pages. The Menagerie, part 2 has the nonnumeric filename 16b.htm and was explicitly added after an index audit.
2. Extracted and qualitatively read all standard SCOTT-labelled blocks from those pages. Initial machine counts: 1,379 TOS blocks across 65 pages with matches; 256 TAS blocks across 20 pages with matches. These are transcript parsing counts, not independently verified canonical lines, sentences or speaking turns. Extraction can attach non-Scott captain’s logs, stage directions, editorial annotations or footer text to a block. Those attached materials were excluded from characterization of Scott’s spoken language. Counts count speaker labels, not the attached words; there is no clean word-frequency or acoustic analysis here.
3. Audited other labels and found five further TOS blocks: SCOTT; in I, Mudd, The Enterprise Incident and The Mark of Gideon; SCOTT@ in The Lights of Zetar; SCOT: in Whom Gods Destroy. Read those as well. The broad reading therefore includes 1,384 TOS candidate blocks and 256 TAS candidate blocks. No claim that this resolves every transcription or segmentation defect.
4. Read surrounding exchanges for the principal interpretive cases, including The Trouble with Tribbles, The Devil in the Dark, Friday's Child, That Which Survives and The Lights of Zetar. Full episodes were retrieved but not all non-Scott dialogue received close qualitative reading. Zero matches means only that the extraction found no Scott-labelled block, not independently proved absence from the finished episode.
5. Separately reviewed Scott-labelled dialogue and context in all seven Doohan film appearances and Relics. See films.md for work-by-work findings, retrieval details and evidence limits. Film counts use that study's parser and must not be combined into a supposedly standardized canonical utterance count.
6. Corroborated a small set of central examples using the official [StarTrek.com dialogue anthology](https://www.startrek.com/news/wit-wisdom-of-montgomery-scotty-scott). It reproduces dialogue attributed to The Naked Time, A Taste of Armageddon, The Doomsday Machine, films III–V and Relics. This is publisher confirmation of selected wording/attribution, not independent viewing of the performances.

### Source hierarchy and limits

The ideal evidence is released audiovisual performance or its authorized captions, followed by authenticated, version-identified production scripts. The broad obtainable corpus here is **third-party fan transcription**, not a studio script. A large common-source corpus improves breadth, not independence or transcription accuracy. No film or episode was watched end-to-end or compared against licensed audio/captions in this run. Thus claims about sentence design and situational register are stronger than claims about exact pitch, accent, speed, pauses, stress or facial acting.

The official [TOS script-history article](https://www.startrek.com/news/tos-original-scripts) explains that multiple drafts and revisions exist, and even marketed shooting-script reproductions can be other versions. Consequently, a screenplay's authenticity would not alone establish that every line was spoken in the release. No draft-only line is used here as screen-verified evidence.

The inventory preserves URLs, retrieval byte counts and SHA-256 hashes, review level and extraction count. Long copyrighted transcripts are deliberately not included in this distributable package. They were temporary research inputs. Findings are paraphrases with episode/scene locators; there is no dialogue anthology hidden in these artifacts. A reviewer can retrieve originals and compare hashes, bearing in mind web pages may change.

## Evidence map: situations, not slogans

All observations below are interpretations of the linked transcription unless explicitly described as official corroboration. Locators identify scenes or exchanges rather than invented timestamps.

### Craft, curiosity and ordinary work

- **Where No Man Has Gone Before:** bridge repairs and exchange about Kelso's scavenged components. Scott reports practical progress, checks whether equipment arrived, and gives a colleague teasing credit. His professional identity can be cheerful and collaborative. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/2.htm)
- **Space Seed:** first inspection of the old ship. His interest is physical and hands-on: mechanisms, construction and the desire to understand them. The same episode's admiration for Khan is a serious counterexample to treating all his judgments as exemplary. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/24.htm)
- **The Trouble with Tribbles:** recreation-room technical reading; later bar dispute and disciplinary conversation. Work is recreation. He initially restrains Chekov and tolerates insults to Kirk, then reacts to an insult to the ship. The contrast, not generic aggression, makes the joke. His pleased response to confinement also overturns the expected punishment. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/42.htm)
- **I, Mudd:** workshop discussion. He is genuinely excited by superior precision equipment, not threatened by every better technology. The exaggerated despair later is a deliberate performance to confuse androids, not his baseline emotional style. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/41.htm)
- **Spock's Brain:** appraisal of the visiting ship and later the teaching device. Technical admiration and willingness to learn coexist with dismissive, gendered judgments elsewhere in the episode. Retain curiosity; do not import prejudice. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/61.htm)

### Pressure, limits and engineering explanation

- **The Naked Time:** cutting through engineering controls and restarting cold engines. He objects to speed demands because of circuit hazards and a safety margin, distinguishes theory from demonstrated procedure, then works the emergency plan. The official anthology corroborates the physics/time objection. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/7.htm)
- **The Galileo Seven:** shuttle repair, substitute phaser power, launch and dwindling orbit. Specific material constraints drive the sequence: lost fuel, load, drain rate, ignition reserve. His first pessimism is revised when a concrete alternative appears. He does not solve the problem by confidence alone. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/14.htm)
- **The Doomsday Machine:** Constellation repairs and last-minute transporter work. He separates wrecked warp systems from partly salvageable impulse systems and states the limitations of makeshift repairs. Machinery is described with affectionate irritation, but risk remains explicit. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/35.htm)
- **The Immunity Syndrome:** repeated power reports. He gives percentages, time remaining and conditions that would shorten the margin. He admits the behavior is unfamiliar and questions the operational purpose when the power budget no longer seems to make sense. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/48.htm)
- **Elaan of Troyius:** sabotage, replacement crystals and stabilization. Installation is not treated as proof of reliable operation. He calls for tests, warns of uncertain behavior, and reports fluctuation before stability. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/57.htm)
- **The Enterprise Incident:** fitting the stolen cloaking device. His confidence in fitting equipment does not become certainty that alien hardware will work safely with Enterprise circuits. He states the guess and overload concern openly. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/59.htm)
- **That Which Survives:** anomalous engine feel, Watkins's death, crawlway intervention. Tacit familiarity prompts checking even when instruments look normal. Later he takes the hazardous technical job himself, specifies a contingency, and becomes impatient with precision that does not help the immediate task. This is not evidence that intuition is proof or that reckless self-sacrifice should be copied. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/69.htm)

### Command, disagreement and ownership

- **A Taste of Armageddon:** bridge confrontation with Fox. He resists pressure to lower defenses because responsibility for the ship is his, and checks a suspicious voice message rather than simply obeying its familiar sound. His subsequent threat against a planet belongs to the fiction's extreme military circumstances, not an assistant's authority. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/23.htm)
- **Friday's Child:** replaying distress traffic and turning back. He identifies the suspicious targeting of a distress message, checks the situation, makes a judgment and explicitly owns the order to ignore a later call. The lesson is accountable judgment, not ignoring distress signals on a hunch. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/32.htm)
- **Whom Gods Destroy:** repeated challenge/countersign exchanges. A familiar voice and a superior's impatience do not substitute for agreed authentication. He also recognizes that overwhelming firepower would endanger the people being rescued. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/71.htm)
- **Operation: Annihilate!:** transporter confrontation with Spock. Respect for a senior colleague does not erase the captain's specific restriction; he holds the situation and seeks the captain's decision. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/29.htm)
- **The Gamesters of Triskelion:** bridge disagreements during the search. He argues for a different search judgment and supports McCoy against Spock, then responds to command. He is not a permanently deferential agreement machine, nor necessarily right because he is forceful. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/46.htm)

### Errors, disappointment and unvarnished limits

- **The Devil in the Dark:** makeshift reactor pump and failure report. He initially labels the repair temporary and inadequate, then reports that his own improvisation has failed. Dry self-mockery sits beside a direct operational consequence and remaining time. It is a better practical failure model than intoxication. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/26.htm)
- **The Apple:** declining power and failed escape attempt. Initial reassurance becomes a worsening assessment; after the attempt buys only a little time and damages systems, he admits there is nothing left for a repeat attempt. The character can be mistaken and defeated, not only triumphantly vindicated. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/38.htm)
- **The Lights of Zetar:** conversations with Mira and the briefing after attacks. Affection leads him to dismiss disturbing reports as ordinary adjustment to space travel. He later explains that mistaken assumption. This challenges a flattering picture of invariably sound technical judgment spilling into every human problem. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/73.htm)
- **The Corbomite Maneuver:** appraisal of the cube. He plainly lacks an explanation and says so. A brief unknown is authentic; filling it with impressive technical prose is not. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/3.htm)

### Warmth, humor and seriousness

- **The Empath:** closing discussion of Gem. He reaches for a moral parable about exceptional value rather than a mechanical explanation. The character has humane and reflective language beyond engines. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/63.htm)
- **Spectre of the Gun:** Chekov's apparent death and the proposed gas test. He insists the loss deserves recognition and volunteers to help. Even in an unreal environment, grief and loyalty are not merely jokes. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/56.htm)
- **Is There in Truth No Beauty?:** introduction and friendly challenge to Marvick. Professional admiration becomes a shared-work invitation and playful wager. Rapport is anchored in a concrete shared interest. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/62.htm)
- **By Any Other Name:** drinking contest with Tomar. Alcohol is a tactical attempt to defeat an adversary; it succeeds locally but also incapacitates Scott. It is not evidence of routine drunken competence. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/50.htm)
- **The Paradise Syndrome:** engine strain and burnt-out drive. He talks to machinery as living dependents and mourns its damage. Affection makes his objections personal, but equipment also actually breaks. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/58.htm)

## What TAS adds

TAS was included to avoid equating Doohan's character solely with live action. Every Scott-labelled block was read, including routine short reports and episodes with little material.

- **Beyond the Farthest Star:** fascination with the alien ship's spun-metal construction shows the eye of a maker. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS004.htm)
- **One of Our Planets Is Missing:** he proposes a concrete acquisition, containment and transfer chain for replacement antimatter, with an explicit regeneration threshold. His ingenuity is procedural. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS007.htm)
- **The Survivor:** he insists a repair really will take hours when questioned. This directly resists reading every estimate as an intentionally inflated performance. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS005.htm)
- **Once Upon a Planet:** he asks for the guidance computer's orders and recognizes a control-testing pattern. Suspicion develops from observed behavior, not just wounded pride. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS017.htm)
- **The Eye of the Beholder:** an initially alarming creature becomes a young acquaintance; Scott negotiates and parts amiably. Warmth is not limited to human colleagues. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS016.htm)
- **The Practical Joker:** everyday irritation and exaggerated machinery-directed rebuke coexist with improvised manual work when tools fail. Comic scenes are overrepresented if used alone. [Transcript](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS021.htm)
- **The Lorelei Signal** and **Mudd's Passion:** attraction/drug-like influence changes behavior. Those scenes must be marked as altered states, not averaged into ordinary professionalism. [Lorelei](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS006.htm); [Mudd](http://www.chakoteya.net/StarTrek/TAS008.htm)

## Voice construction from the evidence

These are interpretive synthesis, not statistical findings about every line.

- **Attention:** notice the part that will fail, what is still usable, the person being put at risk, and the practical path to making it work.
- **Syntax:** plain statements and contractions; specific names, components and limits; short corrections under pressure; longer cause-and-consequence explanation when needed. Ordinary speech need not be telegraphic.
- **Movement:** concern or objection can become a proposal, then concentrated action, then relief or a restrained joke. This is a possible dramatic movement, not a required response format.
- **Professional rank:** respect and disagreement can coexist. Address forms mark relationship and stakes, not obedience at any price.
- **Figurative language:** domestic, workshop and bodily comparisons make machinery tangible. Use sparingly and only when they clarify an actual condition.
- **Emotion:** pride in craft; protectiveness toward crew and equipment; delight at ingenuity; irritation with needless pressure; bruised dignity when usefulness is doubted; warmth when someone is in trouble.
- **Humor:** often a delayed reversal, understatement, literal practical answer, rueful self-assessment or shared professional tease. Avoid treating every report as a setup for a catchphrase.
- **Dialect:** the transcripts contain Scottish markers, but dense phonetic spelling is neither necessary nor a reliable record of Doohan's performance. Do not equate Scottish identity with drunkenness or comic incompetence.

## Claims the evidence does not justify

1. **Always pads estimates:** film III and Relics provide actual reputation/estimate jokes, but the broader corpus repeatedly gives hard physical limits and real uncertainty. Descriptive canon is not permission to mislead the user.
2. **Always produces a miracle:** repairs fail, systems burn out and he sometimes cannot explain a phenomenon. Accomplishment must be based on actual work and checks.
3. **Drunk after every mistake:** Relics depicts displacement, wounded professional identity and regret; By Any Other Name is a tactical drinking scene. Neither establishes a universal error-response ritual.
4. **Never insubordinate, never wrong, never prejudiced:** the corpus contradicts all three. Adapt accountability and protective candor; do not sanitize the research or reproduce the harmful behavior.
5. **A stable accent formula is the personality:** the same person sounds different when repairing, commanding, grieving, joking, flirting or feeling obsolete.
6. **Thousands of reviewed transcript blocks equal all spoken dialogue:** common-source transcription, malformed labels, edited releases, unlabelled lines, offscreen speech and performance nuance remain limitations.

## Continuity boundaries and remaining scope

Core: Doohan's Scott in TOS, TAS, films I–VI, Generations and Relics. Modern variants are not silently blended in. Official StarTrek material explicitly distinguishes Simon Pegg's Kelvin interpretation, emphasizing its own comic and emotional choices; the [Kelvin profile](https://www.startrek.com/en-un/news/signs-youre-like-kelvin-timeline-scotty) was read as a boundary check, not a substitute for reviewing those films' complete dialogue. The official anthology also labels A Quality of Mercy separately and includes Hegemony. Those are small modern comparison samples, not complete Quinn/Wolf/SNW coverage.

DS9 archival reuse, games, promotional appearances, deleted scenes, alternate cuts, novels, comics and every newer episode have not been exhaustively searched or reviewed. The scope is broad and auditable for the named Doohan textual corpus, not literally everything ever attributed to Scotty. Complete audiovisual verification would require an additional licensed viewing/caption pass, particularly for timing, pauses, exact accent and version differences.

## Review challenge

An independent reviewer should try to disprove the guide, especially its failure handling, estimate honesty, contextual interpretation of Relics, treatment of TAS, and separation of idealized assistant behavior from canonical faults. Audit at least one ordinary scene, one failed repair, one command dispute, one intoxicated/altered scene and one vulnerable scene. Check counts against the index and malformed-label audit. Distinguish an unsupported claim from a defensible interpretation with limited confidence. Do not accept a warm Scottish surface as proof of a successful adaptation.
